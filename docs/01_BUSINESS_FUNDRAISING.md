# 01 — Business & Fundraising Guide
*Audience: founder with no prior fundraising experience. Everything here is Ethiopia-and-ADERA-specific, not generic startup advice.*

## 1. How ADERA makes money (recap → profitability path)
Four sequenced streams (master plan §4): subscriptions (USD + ETB) → marketplace engagement fees (10%) → tender-posting fees → later doc-distribution/JV fees. **Profitability logic:** infra burn is ~$20–40/mo; contribution margin per Global Pro sub ≈ $72. So profitability is not a scale question — it's a *distribution* question. The business is default-alive at ~1 subscriber and meaningfully profitable at ~30–50 mixed subs, long before investors are mathematically necessary. That fact is leverage: **you raise to go faster, not to survive** — say exactly that in pitches.

## 2. Fundraising terminology — the 15 terms you actually need
- **Equity:** ownership percentage of the company.
- **Valuation:** agreed worth of the company. **Pre-money** = value before the investment; **post-money** = pre-money + cash invested.
- **Dilution:** your % shrinking when new shares are issued. Example: you own 100%. Investor puts **$50k at $450k pre-money** → post-money $500k → investor owns 50/500 = **10%**, you now own 90%.
- **SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity):** investor gives money now, gets shares later at the next priced round, usually with a **valuation cap** (max valuation their money converts at) and/or **discount** (e.g., 20% cheaper than the round price). Standard for pre-seed because it's fast and cheap (no lawyers negotiating a full round).
- **Convertible note:** like a SAFE but legally a loan (has interest + maturity date). Prefer SAFE if offered a choice.
- **Term sheet:** the 2–5 page summary of deal terms before legal documents. Non-binding mostly, but the negotiation happens here.
- **Cap table:** the spreadsheet of who owns what %. Keep yours from day one (one sheet: name, shares, %, instrument, date).
- **Vesting:** shares earned over time (standard: 4 years, 1-year cliff). Investors will require founder vesting — it's normal, not an insult.
- **Runway:** months of survival at current burn = cash ÷ monthly burn.
- **MRR/ARR:** monthly/annual recurring revenue. **CAC:** cost to acquire a customer. **LTV:** lifetime value of a customer. **Churn:** % of customers who leave per month. Investors will ask for all four — instrument them from the first paying user (03 §7).
- **TAM/SAM/SOM:** total / serviceable / obtainable market. ADERA's honest version: TAM = ~$9B/yr Ethiopian procurement flow context; SAM = tender-intelligence + facilitation spend of diaspora/foreign + local bidders; SOM year-1 = 150–300 paying (master plan §1).
- **Traction:** proof people want it — revenue, retention, engagement. In Ethiopia's early ecosystem, *any* real MRR puts you ahead of most applicants.

## 3. How much to raise, and for what percent
Rules of thumb (pre-seed, East Africa): raise **12–18 months of planned spend**, give up **8–15% total** at this stage; never exceed ~20% pre-seed or your cap table is damaged for later rounds. ADERA-specific math: growth budget = founder stipend (~$300–500/mo so you can work full-time post-graduation) + marketing ($200–400/mo) + facilitator-ops/legal ($100–200/mo) + infra ≈ **$8k–15k for 12–18 months** → that's a **$10k–25k raise at a $100k–250k valuation cap**, i.e., angel-check territory, not VC. Alternative that costs 0%: **grants and competitions** (below). Recommended order: gates + first revenue → grants/competitions → angels → institutional only at scale.

## 4. Where to find money in/for Ethiopia (verified via research Jun–2026; re-verify terms when applying)
- **EASE platform (platform.ease.et):** verified directory of Ethiopian investors/ESOs + direct program applications — register first, it's the ecosystem's front door.
- **Incubators/hubs:** **iceaddis** (Ethiopia's first innovation hub; coaching, investment-prep), **blueMoon** (seed incubator — agri-focused, but network is broad), Orbit Innovation Hub, xHub Addis.
- **Angels:** **Addis Ababa Angels** (~$2M into 12 startups 2023–25), **RENEW / Impact Angel network**, Addis Alemayehou (251 Communications), diaspora angels (note: diaspora fund a large share of Ethiopian early-stage — your product story doubles as your investor story).
- **Government:** MInT ecosystem support; **Innovation & Technology Fund** (reported up to $50k early-stage — verify current program); **Ethiopian Startup Act** (proposed tax breaks/grants — track status, don't plan on it).
- **Pan-African (apply-from-Ethiopia):** **Accelerate Africa** (Future Africa; $250–500k potential, no fee, no upfront equity to apply — deadline-driven, check cycle), Village Capital, VC4A, Startup Map Africa (curated grants/accelerators list), Google for Startups Africa.
- **Student channels (you're ASTU 3rd-year — use this):** Hult Prize (campus chapter route), ASTU innovation/incubation center, national student competitions via MInT. Student-founder framing + working product is a strong wedge in these.

## 5. The pitch — deck outline with ADERA-specific content
10 slides, one idea each: **(1) Title** — "ADERA: the trusted bridge to Ethiopian tenders" + one traction number. **(2) Problem** — a diaspora firm can legally bid but can't: know eligibility, handle CPOs, or trust a stranger in Addis; local SMEs drown in category dumps. **(3) Solution** — AI matching with cited eligibility verdicts + vetted facilitator marketplace + proof-of-submission. Show the eligibility-chips screen. **(4) Why now** — e-GP $9B/yr digitization, Proclamation 1333/2024, diaspora policy push. **(5) Market** — TAM/SAM/SOM honest version (§2). **(6) Product demo** — 60-second flow: profile → match+why → "Can we win this?" brief → facilitator engagement → stamped proof. **(7) Business model** — the 4 streams + unit economics ($72 margin/sub; 10% take). **(8) Competition** — the 2×2: AI-native ↔ listing-only vs local-only ↔ cross-border; ADERA alone in the top-right; name Getchereta and TendersOnTime honestly. **(9) Traction & roadmap** — gates passed, paying users, facilitator LOIs; phase plan. **(10) Ask** — amount, use of funds (3 lines), milestone it buys. 
**What makes investors lean in:** paying customers in USD from a solo student founder; a moat that isn't the model (law corpus + facilitator network + proof artifacts); founder-market fit (you're the bridge persona). **Red flags to avoid:** "no competition" claims (you know better), hockey-stick charts with no basis, raising before gates pass.

## 6. Getting government, facilitators, and posters to *want* in
- **Government:** you are a transparency ally, not a disruptor — ADERA widens participation in public procurement (their stated goal) and sends bidders *to* e-GP. Path: MInT ecosystem events → PPA/e-GP team intro → offer aggregate insight reports (which sectors get too few bidders) as a public good. Never scrape-fight the government; integrate.
- **Facilitators:** their "investment" is participation, not equity — free qualified foreign leads in exchange for vetting and service standards. Pitch: "we replace your marketing budget." LOIs (Gate G-FAC) are your social proof to investors too.
- **Posters:** free beta + distribution report ("your tender reached 214 matching firms") converts them into paying repeat posters; a verified-business badge is reputational value they can't get on newspaper classifieds.

## 7. Process mechanics when someone says yes
Warm intro or EASE/OpenVC cold email (3 sentences + deck link) → 30-min call (them qualifying you) → follow-ups with data-room folder: deck, master plan, demo video, metrics sheet, cap table, registration docs → term sheet → sign SAFE → money lands in the **business** account (never personal — see 02). Take your time reading anything binding; "standard terms" still get read. If a term confuses you, ask the investor to explain it in writing — reasonable ones always will.
