
There are **two different situations**:

1. **Modern platform (like Ethiopia's e-GP)** → online bidding exists.
    
2. **Old/manual platform** → no online bidding.
    

The facilitator's job is much bigger when there is **no online bidding**.

Let's compare them.

## One important point

Even if there is **no online bidding**, a facilitator **should not**:

- Change the company's technical proposal.
    
- Sign documents without authorization.
    
- Claim they can influence the evaluation.
    
- Offer bribes or improper payments.
    
- Promise that the company will win.
    

Their legitimate role is to **provide administrative, logistical, translation, compliance, and communication support**, not to influence the procurement decision.



## online bid issues

**e-GP removes the need for a person to carry papers and talk to government offices. But e-GP cannot replace banks and legal authorities.**

Think of a foreign company:

> A construction company in Germany wants to build a road project in Ethiopia.

They find the tender in e-GP and want to participate.

There are two separate worlds:

```
                 DIGITAL WORLD
                 (e-GP)

Company  ------------------>  Government
       Submit tender online


                 REAL WORLD

Company  -----> Bank
Company  -----> Embassy / Legal authorities
```

The second part is where the difficulties happen.

# 1. Bank Guarantee / Bid Security

## First: What problem is the government trying to solve?

Imagine Ethiopia publishes this tender:

```
Tender:
Construction of a Highway

Estimated project:
$10 million

Required bid security:
$100,000
```

Many companies submit bids.

Company A says:

> "I can build this road for $8 million."

Company B says:

> "I can build it for $8.5 million."

Government evaluates and selects Company A.

Then Company A says:

> "Sorry, we changed our mind. We don't want to do it."

Now the government has a problem:

- They wasted time.
    
- They need to restart the process.
    
- Other companies may have lost interest.
    

So the government says:

> "Before you bid, prove that you are serious."

That proof is called **bid security**.

---

# What exactly is a bid security?

A bid security is a promise from a bank:

> "If this company behaves wrongly during the tender process, we (the bank) will pay the government a certain amount."

It is not the payment for the project.

It is only a guarantee.

Example:

```
Company ABC wants a tender.

Government requires:
Bid security = $100,000


Bank says:

"We guarantee ABC's $100,000 security."

```

---

   |

It creates trust.
